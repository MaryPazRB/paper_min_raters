ANALYSIS NOTES AND OUTPUT PROVENANCE

The current implementation is index2.R. Historical reviewer responses and older
figures were moved to the ignored local archive because some numbers used an
older effect definition. Use the current tables as the numerical reference.
The reviewer3_ prefix identifies distribution diagnostics added during revision;
it does not mean those current CSV outputs are obsolete.

Precision: k_min_cv = max(2, ceiling((sd / (mu * cv_target))^2)), with unaided
rater-level CCCs. This targets relative standard error of the estimated mean:
sd / (mu * sqrt(k)). Historical filenames retain the CV terminology. The app
labels this RSE and defaults to an operational floor of three raters.

Power: the representative baseline is mean unaided CCC among complete rater
pairs with both CCCs strictly inside (-1, 1). It may differ from mu, which uses
all valid unaided raters. Exact boundary pairs are counted and excluded rather
than clipped. Positive improvements have target = baseline + delta_ccc and
contrast delta_z = atanh(target) - atanh(baseline). Targets at or above 1 are
infeasible. The observed SD of transformed paired differences is held fixed
across the four sensitivity effects (0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20).

The main scenario uses delta_ccc = 0.10, alpha = 0.05, two-sided power = 0.80,
and a precision target of 0.10. Two-sided power counts both rejection tails.
Infeasible effects have NA for k_power and k_final. A feasible smaller effect
must remain explicitly labeled when reported; it is not a replacement for an
infeasible main scenario. Representative-baseline contrasts are not the mean
of individual transformed contrasts and do not imply equal attainable gains
for every rater.

Q-Q plots and normality summaries describe observed raw and transformed paired
differences. Non-significant normality tests do not establish normality.
Bootstrap simulations resample whole paired rater differences conditional on
the observed specimens and empirical rater distributions. Match study and
delta_ccc to select a scenario. The analysis does not resample leaves or validate
prospective power, recruitment, learning effects, or independence of raters.
The sequential application stopping rule is a planning heuristic.

Reference-severity histograms count repeated rater observations once per image
and reference set. Gerbera has 30 images measured under two reference sets;
both sets are retained (60 measurements), without inferring laboratory names.
The histogram caption and CSV exports document those reference measurements.

supplementary_table_S1.csv is generated by index2.R from its sensitivity results.
supplementary_table_S1.docx is an existing formatted artifact, not automatically
regenerated. App JPGs are historical screenshots. app_example_history.csv is
regenerated by tests/test_app.R. Figures and numerical CSVs are regenerated by
the documented scripts. Original files are preserved with SHA256 hashes under
_local_archive/before_github_cleanup_20261006/.

CLEANUP VALIDATION (2026-10-06)

The main analysis, histogram generation, standalone supplementary Q-Q command,
and app statistical/input/controller/Shiny server checks completed successfully
with R 4.5.2 on Windows. The deterministic numeric outputs and worked app history
match the archived originals to a comparison tolerance of 1e-12. The supplementary
CSV now uses the existing power-figure disease labels (including an ASCII hyphen
in Tobacco-Xylella). The retained Word table was not changed.

The regenerated bootstrap differs slightly from its archived version: at most
one valid replicate per scenario, 0.000185 in empirical type-I error, and 0.000004
in empirical power. The same seed, resampling code, and analytical method were
used; these tiny differences are consistent with platform floating-point effects
when classifying simulated samples with almost zero variance. The original output
remains in the archive. R and package versions are in session_info.txt.
